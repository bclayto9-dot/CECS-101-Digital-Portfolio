POV Statement Activity
Your POV Statement is the foundation of your entire Define Phase. It crystallizes your self-knowledge into a single, honest, actionable articulation of your academic direction. This is not a formal mission statement — it is a working tool. It will evolve. But you need a starting point, and this activity helps you build one.

Step 1 — Review Your Module 2 Work (10–15 minutes)
Open your previous assignments and read through your key insights from:

Your SWOT Matrix (especially Strengths and Opportunities)
Empathy Map
As you read, highlight or note the three to five themes that appear most consistently. You are looking for patterns — things that showed up in multiple places.

Themes I noticed:

I like working with numbers and solving problems.
Having opportunities to grow in my career is important to me.
I want a career that gives me financial stability and a good income.
I enjoy working with other people and don't want to be isolated all day.
I am still figuring out exactly what career I want, but I have a better idea of the direction I want to go.

Step 2 — Answer the POV Builder Questions
Write honest answers to each of the following questions. Do not overthink — write your first genuine response.

1. Who am I in this moment, academically and personally? (Consider: your background, your identity, your situation as a college student) [Your response here]

I am a college student who is still figuring out exactly what I want to do after graduation, but I know that I want to go into finance. I am planning on majoring in finance with a collateral in accounting. 
Personally, I like working with people and solving problems, and I want a career where I have opportunities to keep moving up.

2. What do I genuinely need from my college education? (Consider: what would make this experience feel meaningful — not just successful) [Your response here]

I need my college education to give me skills that I can actually use in a career. 
I want to learn more about finance and accounting while also getting experience with things like Excel, Bloomberg, and analyzing financial information. 
I also want my degree to give me different career options instead of limiting me to one specific job.

3. What is the most important thing I learned about myself in Module 2? (Consider: something surprising, clarifying, or that shifted how you see yourself) [Your response here]

The most important thing I learned is that I don't have to know exactly what job I want right now. I have realized that I care more about finding a general career direction that fits my strengths and gives me room to grow.

4. What kind of work energizes me? What drains me? (Consider: from your Empathy Map and Ikigai reflection) 

Work that involves solving problems, working with numbers, making decisions, and interacting with other people energizes me.
I also like having some variety in what I am doing. Work that is extremely repetitive or where I am doing the exact same thing every day would probably drain me.

5. What problem or challenge do I most want to contribute to solving in the world? (Consider: even a vague answer is useful here) 

I want to help businesses make better financial decisions. I think helping a company understand its finances, use its money effectively, and make decisions that allow it to grow would be something I would enjoy doing.

Step 3 — Draft Your POV Statement
Use the following template to draft your POV Statement. Write it in your own voice — not in formal or academic language. Write at least two drafts of your POV Statement and choose the one that feels most honest and most useful.

"I am [brief description of who you are — background, identity, situation]. I need [a genuine academic or career need you identified]. Because [the most important insight from your self-assessment work]."

Example:

"I am a first-generation college student from a small town who has always been drawn to science but also deeply values connection to people and community. I need a degree path that lets me combine analytical thinking with direct human impact, because I have learned that I lose motivation and meaning when my work feels disconnected from real people's lives."

Draft 1

I am a college student who is still figuring out exactly where I want my career to go, but I know I am interested in finance and accounting.
I need a degree path that gives me useful financial skills and multiple career opportunities because I have learned that having room to grow and not being stuck doing the same thing is important to me.

Draft 2

I am a college student interested in finance who wants a career that involves problem solving, working with numbers, and interacting with people.
I need a degree that prepares me for different opportunities in the financial world because I have learned that I value career growth, financial stability, and having options for where my career can take me.

Final POV Statement
(Choose the draft — or a refined combination — that feels most honest and most useful.)

I am a college student interested in finance who is still figuring out exactly what career I want after graduation. 
I need a degree path that gives me useful financial and accounting skills while also giving me different career opportunities. 
This is important because I have learned that I want a career with room to grow, financial stability, and work that keeps me challenged.

Step 4 — Reflect

What was difficult about writing your POV Statement?
What did you discover or clarify in the process?
Does your POV Statement surprise you in any way?
How does it connect to your Module 2 findings?

Through this process, I realized that I don't necessarily need to know the exact position yet.
